A ritual is an intentional act set apart from ordinary life — a sequence of actions performed with attention and meaning. Lighting a candle while thinking about your grandmother is a ritual. So is a full moon circle with incense and chanting. The scale doesn't matter; the intention does.

What makes something a ritual rather than a habit is **consciousness**: you know why you're doing it, you've marked the moment as special, and you're present for it. Brushing your teeth is a habit. Washing your hands slowly before an important conversation, thinking "I release the day," is a ritual.

**The anatomy of a simple ritual:**
1. **Set the space:** tidy a little, dim the lights, light a candle or incense — signal to yourself that this time is different.
2. **State the intention:** say aloud what this is for. "I light this candle for clarity about my decision."
3. **The act:** the heart of it — meditation, writing, holding a stone, speaking words, sitting in silence.
4. **Close it:** thank, blow out the candle, tidy. Rituals need endings; the closing tells your mind it's complete.

**Why it works:** ritual externalizes the inner life. Writing the intention, lighting the flame, speaking the words — each step moves something abstract into the physical world, where it feels real and actionable. Psychologists call this "embodied cognition." Practitioners just call it magic.

**Tonight:** Light one candle. Say one true sentence about what you need. Sit with the flame for five minutes. Blow it out. You've performed a ritual.
