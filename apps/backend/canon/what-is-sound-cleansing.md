Sound cleansing uses vibration — bells, bowls, chimes, clapping, even the voice — to clear stagnant energy from a space, object, or person. It's the smoke-free alternative practitioners reach for in apartments with strict leases, around smoke-sensitive people, or simply when they prefer it. The principle is intuitive: sound moves air, and moving air moves stuckness.

**Common tools:**
- **Singing bowls** (Tibetan or crystal): the sustained tone is the classic. Strike or circle the rim, then move the bowl slowly through the space.
- **Bells and tingshas:** bright, sharp, excellent for corners and doorways where heaviness settles.
- **Chimes:** hang where breezes move them for passive, ongoing clearing.
- **Clapping:** the folk method — sharp claps around the body or through a room, listening for where the sound goes flat (practitioners say flat spots mark stuck energy; clap until the tone rings clear).
- **Voice:** toning a single sustained note, or humming, vibrates the body from the inside.

**How to do it:** Start at the door, move clockwise through the space, pausing in corners. There's no wrong note — intention does the heavy lifting; the sound is the vehicle.

**Tonight:** Stand in the room you want refreshed. Clap firmly in each corner, listening for the tone. When every corner rings clear, you're done. Two minutes, no supplies, noticeably different air.
