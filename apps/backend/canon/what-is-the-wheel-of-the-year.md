The Wheel of the Year is the cycle of eight seasonal festivals observed in many nature-based traditions — a ritual calendar that follows the sun through the seasons. It's a way of living in rhythm with the natural world rather than on top of it: each festival marks a turning point in light, growth, harvest, or rest.

**The eight festivals, briefly:**
- **Samhain** (Oct 31) — the year's end; honoring ancestors and the thinning veil
- **Yule** (winter solstice) — the return of the light; hope in darkness
- **Imbolc** (Feb 1) — first stirrings; purification and new beginnings
- **Ostara** (spring equinox) — balance; planting, fertility, fresh starts
- **Beltane** (May 1) — passion, vitality, the greening world
- **Litha** (summer solstice) — peak light; celebration and abundance
- **Lammas/Lughnasadh** (Aug 1) — first harvest; gratitude and gathering in
- **Mabon** (autumn equinox) — balance again; thanksgiving, preparation for dark

**Working with it:** You don't need to celebrate all eight. Many practitioners honor just the solstices and equinoxes, or whichever festivals resonate. A Wheel of the Year practice can be as simple as a seasonal altar refresh and a short ritual on each turning — a candle, a walk outdoors, a written reflection on the season's theme.

**Tonight:** Find the nearest upcoming festival. Read about its theme, take a walk outside, and notice where the season actually is — the light, the air, what's growing or letting go. The wheel turns whether you watch or not; watching is the practice.
